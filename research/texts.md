## 5. What the exam does with these texts (real papers 2021-2026, which I read)

Structure seen in every June paper (the taught part): **Α1** (a) Σωστό/Λάθος or multiple choice or matching on the passage, with quotation of evidence in some years (2021, 2024), (b) "to which word does the pronoun refer" (2021 αὐτῶν, οὕτω; 2025 παρ’ ἣν, ὑπὲρ ὧν; 2026 αὐτοῖς, τούτων) or matching. **Β1** an interpretive question on the passage (e.g. 2026: how arts and virtues are born and destroyed, and the teacher's role). **Β2** a "παράλληλο κείμενο" printed in the paper (2025 Orwell *1984*; 2026 M. Stathopoulos; 2023 Anonymus Iamblichi; 2024 Marcus Aurelius) to be compared with the taught text; in 2021 and 2022 B2 was "find four/two language choices" in the passage. **Β3/Β4** Σωστό/Λάθος or matching on the **Εισαγωγή** (2021 Socrates, Plato, Hesiod; 2022 Aristotle life; 2024 Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Heraclitus; 2025 Plato, republic guards, philosopher-kings; 2026 Aristotle), and **etymology** (match ancient words to modern Greek derivatives, or write a cognate: 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026). **Γ** unseen text: translation 20, comprehension 10, **morphology 10 and syntax 10 on the UNSEEN text only** (2026: Lysias; plural/singular change, imperative aorist passive, future infinitive, optative, imperfect; syntax labels; type of conditional → "προσδοκώμενο"). So **grammar/syntax of the taught texts is never examined directly**; what is examined of the taught texts: content and interpretation, referents of pronouns, rhetorical/language choices, and etymology of their words. (The Grammar/Syntax lists on my cards are for the learner's own learning and for building transfer exercises.)
Examples of the Εισαγωγή question style (all answerable from the textbook intro): "Aristotle was born in Miesa and died in Chalcis" (false: Stageira), "Aristotle reviewed Homer's epics for Alexander" (true), "Eudoxus of Cnidus influenced Aristotle positively" (true), "the seventh letter gives information on Plato's life" (true; 2025 offered "15th" as the false version), "the guardians' first stage: gymnastics and music", "philosopher-kings own property" (false), "Xenocrates directed the Academy" (true), "Heraclitus: daimon is character" (true).

## 7. Εισαγωγή fact bank (from the textbook, own words; every item below is examinable by the syllabus)

**Socrates (Δ2, Δ3).** Method: elenchus by questions; *dialectic* = step-by-step refutation of the partner's view, then a joint move to a general truth; *maieutics* = the philosopher does not hand over a theory but, like a midwife (his mother was one), helps the partner bring the truth out; *irony* as a tool of inquiry; he turned philosophy from the sky to human matters; searched for universal definitions with the *inductive method* (ἐπακτικοὶ λόγοι, τὸ ὁρίζεσθαι καθόλου, source: Aristotle *Metaph.* 1078b27); reason, not the senses, leads to truth. His "wisdom" = knowing that he knows nothing (Apology 20-21, the oracle of Delphi). Trial: charged by Meletus (with Anytus and Lycon) with not honouring the city's gods, introducing new daimonia, corrupting the young; Greek religion had no sacred book, dogma or priestly class, so the charge hid political motives (his pupils Alcibiades, Critias, Charmides, both related to Plato, and the Thirty); jury of 500 found him guilty (281 v 220) and sentenced him to death (300 v 201); he proposed free meals in the Prytaneion, then a one-mina fine, raised to 30 by Plato, Crito and friends; one month in prison until the sacred ship returned from Delos; refused escape (Crito); drank hemlock.
**Plato (Ε1).** Born 428/7 of aristocrats (father Ariston; mother Perictione, relative of Solon; uncle Critias and Charmides were among the Thirty). Main source for his life: the **Seventh Letter**. Wrote tragedies and burned them after meeting Socrates. Disgusted by the Thirty and by Socrates's condemnation, he left active politics; after Socrates's death went to Megara (Euclides). Travelled to South Italy and Sicily (c. 398-390), met the Pythagoreans (Archytas) and Dionysius I and Dion; Dionysius expelled him and he nearly became a slave at Aegina; later trips to Syracuse 366 (Dionysius II) and 361 failed; founded the **Academy** (c. 1.5 km from the Dipylon gate, near the gymnasium of Akademos; lasted until 529 AD under Justinian); maths was the prelude to dialectic; tradition: "ἀγεωμέτρητος μηδεὶς εἰσίτω". Pupils: Aristotle, Speusippus (successor), Xenocrates, Philip of Opus.
**Protagoras (Β, Γ).** Two themes: what is virtue, and who can teach it (are sophists suitable?). Socrates' method = dialectic; Protagoras uses three means: myth, lecture, commentary on poets; Plato accepts the myth as symbolic but not as a path to precise knowledge (he uses myths himself, only to complete a proof), mocks the long lecture (persuasion not truth), distrusts written/commented poetry (the text cannot answer). The myth is Protagorean in substance and echoes Hesiod (Prometheus, Pandora; gloomy view of history) and Aeschylus (Prometheus as benefactor; optimistic); Protagoras stresses society: survival = living in a city; all virtue is political; the Greek citizen's virtue is not separate from the person's. Protagoras accepts relativity; Socrates seeks a universal, absolute truth.
**Republic (intro 1, 2, 3, 6, 8, 12, 13, + cave note).** 1: Plato wanted politics, was disappointed (the Thirty 404-403; Socrates condemned 399; Dionysius I); concludes all existing constitutions are corrupt, healthy politics needs philosophers to rule or rulers to philosophize; justice = each does the work he is fitted and educated for. 2: *Politeia or On the Just*; 10 books (not Plato's division); Stephanus pages 327a-621d; written c. 374 BC; dramatic date 421. 3: Socrates and Glaucon go down to the Piraeus for the festival of Bendis, stop at Cephalus's house; speakers: Cephalus, Polemarchus, Thrasymachus, Cleitophon, Glaucon, Adeimantus; Socrates tells it the next day ("Κατέβην χθὲς εἰς Πειραιᾶ"); the question is justice, studied by building an ideal city first. 6: three classes: craftsmen; guardian-auxiliaries; guardian-rulers (really two classes, since rulers are selected from the auxiliaries; mobility between classes exists). 8: education of guardians: first gymnastics + music; ages 20-30 mathematical sciences (arithmetic, geometry, solid geometry, astronomy, harmonics); then **five years of dialectic (30-35)** to see the Good; tests; after 50 they become rulers; art is censored (Homer, Hesiod). 12: philosopher-kings: Athenian democracy ruled by demagogues, offices by lot; ship-of-state allegory (blind, deaf owner, quarrelling crew, true pilot = philosopher); they rule out of duty, own no property and have no family, to be incorruptible. 13: ideal city has four virtues: wise (rulers), brave (auxiliaries), moderate (harmony of classes), and just (each does his own work, no meddling); soul parts: λογιστικόν rules, θυμοειδές helps, ἐπιθυμητικόν obeys; justice = harmony from "γνῶθι σαυτόν"; the definitions refuted: Cephalus (honesty/paying debts), Polemarchus (help friends, harm enemies), Thrasymachus (the interest of the stronger), Glaucon (a hypocritical social contract); Socrates: "τὸ τὰ αὑτοῦ πράττειν" (433a). Cave note: allegory = continued metaphor; prisoners see shadows; the sun = the Good; the freed one must go back down; allusion to Socrates's death.
**Aristotle (Βίος και έργα).** Born 384 in Stageira (Chalcidice), father Nicomachus, court doctor to Amyntas III; at 17 (367) to the Academy; Plato then in Sicily; Eudoxus of Cnidus; Plato's nickname for him: "Νοῦς" and "ὁ ἀναγνώστης"; 20 years in the Academy; works of the first period: *Categories, De interpretatione, Topics, Analytics, Poetics, Rhetoric (bks 1-2), Eudemian Ethics*… ; Assos and Mytilene (Theophrastus), zoology period (*Historia animalium, Parts of animals, De anima*); 343/2 to Macedon at Philip's call, tutor of the 13-year-old Alexander at Mieza, using Homer; 335 back to Athens, teaches in the **Lyceum** (the school became the Peripatos), *Politics, Nicomachean Ethics, parts of Metaphysics*, 158 constitutions; leaves Athens after Alexander's death; dies at Chalcis in 322, aged 63. Xenocrates then led the Academy.
**Nicomachean Ethics (intro).** Name from Aristotle's father or son Nicomachus; Book 1: the highest practical good = *eudaimonia* = "ψυχῆς ἐνέργειά τις κατ’ ἀρετὴν τελείαν" (an activity, not a state); Heraclitus "ἦθος ἀνθρώπῳ δαίμων" and Democritus: happiness is a matter of the soul, not of herds or gold; the soul has a rational part and an irrational part, with a third part that shares both (ἐπιθυμητικόν: the seat of ethical virtues); virtues are intellectual (wisdom, prudence) or ethical; Book 2 deals with ethical virtue.
**Politics (intro).** Ethics is part of politics: Aristotle ends the *NE* by moving to laws and constitutions; Politics: origin of the polis, the citizen, the constitution, kinds of constitutions, government, education; "πόλις" ≈ state, made of rulers and ruled, aims at eudaimonia through self-sufficiency (αὐτάρκεια); overview of books (A household; B critique of ideal and real constitutions; Γ polis, citizen, kinds of constitutions, summation theory; Δ democratic and oligarchic; Ε revolutions; Ζ; Η-Θ the best polis and its education).
