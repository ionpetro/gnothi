Official guidelines (2026-27, Εγκύκλιος 118956/Δ3/2026; PDF https://alfavita.gr/sites/default/files/2026-08/%CE%91%CE%A1%CE%A7%CE%91%CE%99%CE%91_%CE%95%CE%9B%CE%9B_%CE%93%CE%9B_%CE%91%CE%A1%CE%A7%CE%91%CE%99%CE%91-%CE%95%CE%9B%CE%9B_%CE%93%CE%95%CE%9B-2026_2027.pdf) **[READ]**, Γ' ΓΕΛ section, say in substance:
- The Φάκελος has 6 thematic units / 22 teaching units; each has intro texts, interpretive comments, **one reference text**, 1-3 parallel texts, 1-2 self-activity texts.
- Parallel and self-activity texts printed in the Φάκελος are **not examinable** (they only train intertextual and morpho-syntactic reading). The exam can bring any other parallel text, ancient or modern, Greek or foreign (this is exactly what happened: Orwell, Rousseau, Marcus Aurelius, Euripides, Lucretius, Stathopoulos, Plorites...).
- For interpretation students must use: the textbook introductions (as listed in the syllabus), the thematic-unit introductions of the Φάκελος, and the comments printed with each reference text.
- Scoring criteria for an interpretive answer: completeness relative to the above material, clarity, coherence, and **documentation from the reference text**. Students should not just list book comments; they must interpret the passage **for the specific question**, and avoid "far-fetched remarks, display of philosophical or encyclopaedic knowledge, and anything off the point".
- The parallel-text question aims at **comparison with the reference text**; the wording of the question must target specific points of both texts; students should not repeat what they said in the first interpretive question but go deeper; avoid philosophical anachronisms and comparisons beyond a pupil's reach.
- If a comprehension question is open-ended, answer only what is asked, after decoding the text, without drifting into interpretation.
- No word limit exists for interpretive answers but "an indicative word limit would help" (so the committee expects concision).
- Do not teach the introductions by rote or reproduce the textbook authors' phrasing mechanically.

## 2. Anatomy of the taught-text half (what each question really is) [READ: 2021-2026 papers]

The last 6 years of papers (June and September, `research/pastpapers-raw/`) show a stable skeleton for Section Α+Β = **50 marks** (the unseen text is the other 50):

| Part | Marks | What it is | Typical form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Α1 | 10 | comprehension of the reference text, "no commentary" | 6 marks Σωστό/Λάθος (sometimes "justify with a quotation", 2024) or matching/multiple choice; 4 marks word-reference ("to which word does pronoun X refer?", "find four words about το αγαθόν") |
| Β1 | 10 | **interpretive question on the reference text** | "What view does Aristotle express about...", "How are arts and virtues born and what is the teacher's role?", often split 6+4 or 7+3, the second part asks for 2 **linguistic/expressive choices** or a link to another passage |
| Β2 | 10 | **parallel text comparison** | an unseen modern/ancient parallel text; "find one similarity in para 1 and one difference in para 2 and document with passages of both texts" |
| Β3 | 10 | closed question on the **introductions** (life of Aristotle/Plato/Socrates, Republic, EN, Politics) | 5 Σωστό/Λάθος items, or matching, or multiple choice |
| Β4 | 10 | **lexical/etymology question** | see §6 |

The committee (ΟΕΦΕ) described the 2024 questions like this: Β1 "demands good knowledge of the interpretive comments of the Φάκελος and combinatorial thinking"; Β2 (parallel text) "demands combinatorial and critical thinking"; Β3 and Β4 "no difficulty" (`research/pastpapers-raw/2024-oefe-comment.txt`, press release of 4 June 2024) **[READ]**. For 2026 the ΟΕΦΕ said the taught-text answers "could not rest only on the book's comments; extra knowledge was needed", the unseen interpretive question concerned the whole passage, grammar/syntax "expected" (https://www.aftodioikisi.gr/paideia/panelladikes-oi-apantiseis-sta-archaia-ellinika-apo-tin-oefe/ and powergame.gr summary) **[READ-SECONDARY]**. A press report quoted a 55.67% share of candidates below 10 in Ancient Greek 2026 (iefimerida stats) **[SNIPPET, unverified]**; use only as a hint that the course is high-stakes.

A tutor (Δ. Νάτσης, 2024) summarises: "two interpretive questions now, one related to the parallel; minimum two paragraphs for the taught-text one" **[READ-SECONDARY]**, https://filologikosxoleio.gr/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/ΤΕΛΕΥΤΑΙΕΣ-ΟΔΗΓΙΕΣ-ΓΙΑ-ΤΑ-ΑΡΧΑΙΑ-ΠΡΙΝ-ΤΙΣ-ΠΑΝΕΛΛΑΔΙΚΕΣ.pdf

## 3. The introductions: fact sheet for Β3 (closed question) [READ textbook, paraphrased]

All items below were checked against the textbook introductions and against how past papers phrased true/false items. False items in the papers are made by swapping one fact (city, person, number, order) or by an over-general word ("all", "only", "never", "always").

**Socrates (Δ2, Δ3)**
- Born 470/469, son of the stone-mason/sculptor Sophroniscus and the midwife Phaenarete, demos Alopece. Wrote nothing; our sources: Plato (mainly), Xenophon.
- "I know that I know nothing" attitude; Delphic oracle; irony used as a weapon of inquiry (not as atheism).
- Differs from the sophists: their doubt ends in denial of truth; his doubt looks for a deeper, unchanging truth.
- **Dialectic** = gradual, step-by-step refutation of the partner's positions, then a new approach to truth. **Maieutic** = he does not state theories; he helps the partner "give birth" to truth (link to recollection/anamnesis, cf. the cave intro). He "brought philosophy down from the stars to earth" (Aristotle's formulation: after Socrates inquiry into nature stopped and philosophers turned to useful virtue and politics).
- **Inductive method** (ἐπακτικοὶ λόγοι) aims at **universal definitions** (τὸ ὁρίζεσθαι καθόλου) of **ethical** concepts, starting from everyday examples. Exam trap: not "all concepts"; not "deductive".
- Trial: accusers Meletus (a poet), Anytus (tanner/politician) and Lycon (orator); charges: not honouring the city's gods and introducing new divinities (daimonia), and corrupting the young; penalty proposed: death. Real causes: political (his circle included Alcibiades and Critias/Charmides); the religious charge was a pretext (Greek religion had no dogma or priesthood with mediating role; typical charge was impiety). Court: Heliaia of 500 judges, guilty by a moderate majority (the book: 281 vs 220), death penalty by a larger one (300 vs 201). Irony at the penalty phase (proposed meals in the prytaneion). One month in prison because the Delian sacred ship was away; refused to escape (Crito). A paper (June 2021) had the false item "Socrates was suspected of **democratic** sentiments" (the book says **anti**-democratic).

**Plato (Ε1)**
- Born 428/9, aristocratic; father Ariston, mother Perictione; relatives Critias (uncle of the mother) and Charmides. Main autobiographical source: **7th Letter** (not the 15th: false item 2025).
- Abandoned poetry/tragedy after meeting Socrates; after the Thirty and the trial he gave up personal political ambition (so "he was politically active in Athens" is false, key 2025; "from youth to old age he showed special interest in politics" (Sept 2026 item 4) is true by my reading of the book; the Sept 2026 key was not seen).
- After Socrates' death: Megara with Eucleides. First trip to South Italy and Sicily c. 398-390: contact with Pythagoreans (Archytas of Tarentum) and with Dionysius I (tyrant of Syracuse) and Dion; the trip ended badly: he was expelled and nearly sold into slavery in Aegina (so "dramatic developments" is true; 2025 item). Later trips 366 and 361 (Dionysius II).
- Founded the **Academy** after the first Sicilian trip, near the gymnasium of Akademos; ran for 900 years until Justinian closed it in 529 AD. Mathematics as preparation for dialectic ("ἀγεωμέτρητος μηδεὶς εἰσίτω" tradition). Students: Aristotle, Speusippus (nephew and successor), Xenocrates, Philip of Opus.
- Works in dialogue form (except Apology and Letters); chronological groups in the book: youthful (Apology, Crito, Ion, Laches, Charmides, Lysis, **Protagoras**, Euthyphro), mature (Gorgias, Euthydemus, Meno, Phaedo, Symposium, **Republic**...), late (Theaetetus, Sophist, Statesman, Philebus, Timaeus, Critias, Laws). The early dialogues were written in the first decade **after** Socrates' death (Sept 2026 item 5, "written after the death of Socrates", is true by my reading; its items 1, "central place of **isotimia**", and 2, "ideal polity in the **Protagoras**", are false: the theme is justice and the work is the Republic).

**Protagoras (intro Β, Γ)**
- Two themes: (1) what is the nature of virtue; (2) can virtue be taught and are sophists fit to teach it. The dialogue ends open (aporetic); fuller Platonic view comes in the Republic.
- Contrast of methods: Socrates = dialectic (questions that define terms exactly); Protagoras = myth, lecture, interpretation of poets. Plato respects the myth but uses myths only to complete a strict philosophical argument; he mocks the long speech (Socrates "has a bad memory") because a lecture persuades, it does not seek truth; written/poetic authority is mute and cannot answer questions. Protagoras = relativism; Socrates = general absolute truth.
- The myth is shaped by Hesiod (Theogony: Pandora; pessimistic) and Aeschylus (PV: Prometheus as benefactor; optimistic). Protagoras follows the Aeschylean optimism and stresses social organisation: for the Greeks private virtue is not separated from civic virtue; man is a citizen first.

**Republic (intro items 1, 2, 3, 6, 8, 12, 13)**
- (1) Plato's youthful political ambitions; 404 oligarchic coup; Thirty ruled 8 months (404-403); trial of Socrates 399 convinces him that all regimes are corrupt and only philosophers ruling (or rulers philosophising) can fix them; core idea justice = each does the work they are fitted and trained for.
- (2) Written c. 374; 10-book division not Plato's; Stephanus pagination 327a-621d; dramatic date 421.
- (3) Setting: Piraeus, festival of Bendis (Thracian goddess identified with Artemis), house of the metic Cephalus (father of Lysias). Participants: Cephalus, Polemarchus (his son), Thrasymachus (sophist), Cleitophon, Glaucon and Adeimantus (Plato's brothers), Socrates; from book 2 on mainly Socrates, Glaucon, Adeimantus. Theme: nature of justice/injustice and whether the just man is happier; method: build a city in speech. **polis** = city-state, **politeia** = regime/constitution.
- (6) Three classes: producers (craftsmen, farmers, traders), auxiliaries/guardians-soldiers, guardians-rulers; really two classes because rulers come from auxiliaries; social mobility allowed; the "noble lie" (γενναῖον ψεῦδος, 414b-415c): earth-born, gold/silver/iron-bronze souls; the city is destroyed if an iron/bronze guardian guards it.
- (8) Education of the guardians: first stage gymnastics + music (the arts); 20-30 mathematical sciences (arithmetic, geometry, stereometry, astronomy, harmonics); 30-35 dialectic (five years); those who pass the trials become rulers after 50; they enjoy intellectual pleasures and are honoured as heroes. (2025 Β4 item 3: the first two stages were gymnastics+music, as well as the mathematical sciences = **Σωστό** in the key.) Controlled art (Homer/Hesiod excluded for portraying gods badly; imitative art condemned in book 10).
- (12) Philosopher-kings: ship-of-state allegory (blind/deaf owner, quarrelling crew, the true navigator is the philosopher); basileus = ruler, not hereditary monarch; they have knowledge, practical experience, administrative ability, integrity; take office from duty; have **no private property and no family** (2025 item: "philosopher-kings have property" = false).
- (13) Justice: the ideal city is wise (rulers), brave (auxiliaries), temperate (harmony of classes) and just (each class does its own work, no meddling); the soul has the same structure (logistikon, thymoeides, epithymetikon).

**Aristotle's life (5 sections)**
- Born 384 in **Stagira** (Chalcidice), died in **Chalcis** 322 aged 63 (not Mieza: that is where he taught Alexander; 2026 item 1 false). Father **Nicomachus**, doctor at the court of Amyntas III (June 2024 Β4 item 3, key β: Nicomachus, not Amyntas III or Diogenes Laertius). Came to Athens at 17 in 367 while Plato was away on his **second** of three Sicilian trips (the textbook says "second of the three"; June 2023 Β4 item 1 key = "second"; so a statement that he arrived during Plato's **first** trip, as printed in the Sept 2022 and Sept 2024 papers, is Λάθος **[INFERRED from the book and the June 2023 key; I did not find the Sept keys]**). June 2023 Β4 key also shows: the soul has three parts for Aristotle in the book's wording "ἄλογον, λόγον ἔχον, ἐπιθυμητικόν"; the polis consists of **unlike** parts; Theophrastus' school = Peripatos; eudaimonia = activity.
- Met **Eudoxus of Cnidus** at the Academy, who shaped his scientific temperament (2026 item 4 true: positive influence). Plato's nicknames for him: "Nous" and "the reader". 20 years in the Academy; sharp criticism of colleagues (Heraclides, Speusippus, Xenocrates) **and also of Plato** (2026 item 2 false: "except Plato").
- 347 Plato dies, Speusippus heads the Academy, Aristotle leaves (political reasons: anti-Macedonian climate after the fall of Olynthus 348) for Assos (invited by Hermias, tyrant of Atarneus); then Mytilene (meets Theophrastus). 343/2 called by Philip to educate Alexander (13) at Mieza; used the Homeric epics and prepared a new edition of them (2026 item 3 true). 335 returns to Athens, teaches in the **Lyceum** (public gymnasium; Peripatos tradition), Academy then led by Xenocrates. 323 leaves for Chalcis (the house inherited from his mother) and dies there; the anecdote that he did not want Athenians to wrong philosophy a second time.
- Three periods of activity: (1) Academy/Assos: logic (Categories ... Topics, Analytics), Poetics, Rhetoric (books I-II), Eudemian Ethics, Magna Moralia, parts of Physics and Metaphysics that discuss the Ideas; (2) Assos/Mytilene/Macedonia: biological works (History of Animals, Parts of Animals, De anima...); (3) second Athenian period (Lyceum): mature works; the book lists the Eudemian Ethics and Magna Moralia in the first period, so the **Nicomachean Ethics** belongs to the later (third) period **[INFERRED; Sept 2023 Β4 item 3 asked exactly this, key not seen]**. Works split into exoteric (lost, for a wide public) and acroamatic (lecture notes; survived; story of Neleus, Apellicon, Sulla, Andronicus of Rhodes).
- Teleological explanation of the "five true/false" patterns: dates, places, persons, "first/second", "positive/negative influence".

**EN introduction**: the books are called Nicomachean after his father or his son (who died young); Book Α treats the supreme practical good, **eudaimonia = "ψυχῆς ἐνέργειά τις κατ' ἀρετὴν τελείαν"**: an **activity** of the soul, not a state (2023, 2026 items), in accordance with perfect virtue. Backgrounds the book cites: Protagoras' εὐβουλία; Socratic circle's εὐτυχία; Heraclitus "ἦθος ἀνθρώπῳ δαίμων"; Democritus "εὐδαιμονίη οὐκ ἐν βοσκήμασιν οἰκεῖ οὐδὲ ἐν χρυσῷ" (Sept 2021 matching item with Heraclitus/Democritus/Protagoras/Aristotle; 2024 multiple choice item). Soul: rational (λόγον ἔχον) and irrational (ἄλογον); the irrational splits into a nutritive part (no relation to virtue) and one that **participates** in reason (the desiring part: ethical virtues); the purely rational part has the **dianoetic** virtues (sophia, phronesis). Hence two kinds of virtue; Books Β-Ε ethical virtue as mean between two vices; Ζ intellectual; Η continence/incontinence, pleasure; Θ-Ι friendship; Κ pleasure and happiness.

**Politics introduction**: the Ethics ends by announcing "laws and constitutions", so ethics is part of politics: virtues are acquired to be a good citizen. *polis* ≈ our "state", a whole made of unlike parts (rulers and ruled) with the aim of eudaimonia achieved through autarkeia. Contents: Α household/economy; Β critique of existing/ideal constitutions (Plato, Phaleas, Hippodamus; Sparta, Crete, Carthage); Γ citizen, polis, correct constitutions and deviations, summative (additive) theory, ostracism, kingship; Δ democracies/oligarchies; Ε revolutions; Ζ construction/maintenance; Η-Θ best polis and education. The text of Pol. Α1.1-8 is "the polis is the most perfect community".

(Caveat: committee items follow the introductions almost verbatim and flip one word; the app should drill the introductions as short true/false statements with the "flipped" variant shown next to the true one.)

## 5. Question taxonomy and what a top answer contains

### 5.1 Verbs and what they demand (from Β1/Β2 stems 2021-2026)

| Stem verb in the exam | What is graded | Model move |
|---|---|---|
| "Πώς ο Αριστοτέλης συνδέει Χ και Υ;" | the logical link in the text | name the link in one sentence (cause, condition, analogy, opposition), then prove it with 2-3 Greek phrases |
| "Ποια είναι τα μέρη / τα χαρακτηριστικά ...;" | complete list from the passage | list in order; quote one phrase per item; add the book's gloss on each term |
| "Να εντοπίσετε δύο γλωσσικές / εκφραστικές επιλογές με τις οποίες ..." (usually 4 of 10) | two precise devices, each located by a Greek phrase and linked to its effect | device name + quoted phrase + what it achieves (see 5.3) |
| "Πώς σχετίζονται οι όροι ... με ...;" | definitions of the terms from the book comments, then their relation | define each term in 1 sentence (book gloss), then state the relation, then one textual proof |
| "Να παρουσιάσετε τα χαρακτηριστικά του ... στο διδαγμένο και στο παράλληλο" | both texts covered, **specific** passages cited for each | two-column mental table: similarity/difference, each with a passage from **both** texts |
| "Να τεκμηριώσετε με παραπομπές στο κείμενο" | quotations or references to lines/phrases | Greek phrase in «» followed by a short paraphrase |

### 5.2 Anatomy of a top answer (synthesis of: the official guideline criteria; the ΤΘΔΔ indicative answers; ΟΕΦΕ-style keys 2022-2026)

1. **One-sentence thesis** that answers the question as asked (no history, no biography).
2. **Two to three moves**, each a short paragraph: claim from the passage → quotation in Greek («...») → interpretation using the book's gloss on the key term (e.g. what *ἔθος*, *τέχνη*, *δίκη* mean **here**) → link to the question.
3. **Context only when it serves the answer**: e.g. in the 2026 Β1 the keys bring the generative pair γίγνεται-φθείρεται, the repeated εὖ-κακῶς, the analogy of lyre-players and builders, then the contrary-to-fact condition (εἰ γὰρ μὴ οὕτως εἶχεν ... οὐδὲν ἂν ἔδει τοῦ διδάξοντος) to prove that the habit is acquired and that the teacher/lawgiver shapes it.
4. **Close with a one-line conclusion** that restates the answer in the question's terms.
5. **Length**: the question-bank indicative answers run about 290-310 words (the unit-10 sample counted "[309 λέξεις]" for Β1 and "[293 λέξεις]" for Β2); the ΟΕΦΕ-style keys are much longer (they over-answer for the sake of tutors). Official guidance: no fixed limit; a pupil should aim at about 150-250 words for a 10-mark question with 6+4 split, and **at least two paragraphs** (Νάτσης). **[INFERRED]** for the exact number.
6. **Marks split** (when printed): the first part (6-7) tests understanding of the passage, the second (3-4) tests a precise device or a link; do not write a big essay for the 3-4 marks.
7. **Do not**: retell the myth, list encyclopedic facts, or repeat the points of Β1 inside Β2 (official guideline); do not use philosophical anachronisms (e.g. calling the cave "Matrix", Rousseau-style social contract) beyond what the question needs.

### 5.3 Language/expression devices the keys accept (to cite in "2 γλωσσικές επιλογές")

From the ΟΕΦΕ-style/ΤΘΔΔ answers and the Φάκελος "Γ. Για τη γλώσσα του κειμένου" activities: **αντίθεση** (e.g. ζῆν / εὖ ζῆν; φύσει / ἔθει; ὑπερβολή / ἔλλειψις), **παρομοίωση / αναλογία / παράδειγμα** (κιθαρισταί-οἰκοδόμοι; συμφορητὰ δεῖπνα; ὥσπερ ἐν κρατῆρι; ὥσπερ ἀγέλης), **μεταφορά / αλληγορία** (ἀπείκασον, κρατήρ, ὄναρ, νόσος πόλεως), **επανάληψη** (ἔθος, νέμω, δεῖ), **τριάδες και αντιθετικά ζεύγη**, **ρητορική ερώτηση / διάλογος** (Hermes asks Zeus), **υποθετικός λόγος αντίθετου του πραγματικού** (εἰ γὰρ μὴ οὕτως εἶχεν...), **απρόσωπα ρήματα και ρηματικά επίθετα** (δεῖ, -τέον: obligation), **έναρθρο απαρέμφατο** (τὸ θαυμάζειν, τὸ φεύγειν: nominal verb), **αποδεικτικοί σύνδεσμοι** (γάρ, ὥστε, διό, ἄρα), **πρόσωπο** (first plural ἡμᾶς in «ὁμοίους ἡμῖν»: Plato includes the reader), **ασύνδετο** (γυμνὸν ... ἀνυπόδητον ... ἄστρωτον ... ἄοπλον), **στερητικό α-** (ἄοπλος, ἀπαιδευσία, ἄχρηστος), **συναισθητά επίθετα και εικόνες φωτός/σκότους**, **παρένθεση**, **ηχηρή λέξη-κλειδί** (ἄτοπος).
Principle: **name the device, quote the Greek, say what effect/meaning it carries**. A device named without a quotation scores little.

### 5.4 The parallel-text question (Β2): a method that matches the guideline

- Read the question; it will name a **paragraph** and a **passage** (2025: "para 1 → similarity; para 2 → difference"; 2026: parallel text vs «Οὕτω δὴ ... ἄδικοι»).
- Write: *thesis (similarity or difference in one clause) → what the parallel text says (with a quote from it) → what the taught text says (with a Greek quote) → why they match/differ (one line of reasoning)*.
- Typical axes that recur: appearance vs reality; individual vs community; habit vs nature; knowledge vs power; intellectuals' responsibility (see 2026 Sept); freedom/justice as social virtue; education as formation of citizens.
- Differences are usually "scope" (the ancient text is about a polis, the modern about states/individual rights), "foundation" (nature/teleology vs law/experience), "method" (education vs coercion).
- Keep each side to the point asked; avoid summarising both texts fully.

### 5.5 Comprehension Α1 tips (4+6 marks)

- Σ/Λ: the false items alter one element; if "justify" is asked (2024, ΤΘΔΔ), quote the **Greek phrase** that proves it (ΤΘΔΔ marks: 3 for truth value, 3 for the quotation).
- "Σε ποια λέξη αναφέρεται η αντωνυμία": find the antecedent in the sentence before (2025: παρ' ἣν → ὁδόν; ὑπὲρ ὧν → παραφράγματα; 2026 key: «ἐν αὐτοῖς» → «τὰ ἐν τοῖς συναλλάγμασι, τὰ ἐν τοῖς δεινοῖς, τὰ περὶ τὰς ἐπιθυμίας, τὰς περὶ τὰς ὀργάς»; «τούτων» → «τὰς ἐνεργείας»).
- "Find four words about το ἀγαθόν" (Sept 2026): list the four noun/pronoun references (τὸ μάθημα..., τὸ ἀγαθόν, ἐκείνην τὴν ἀνάβασιν, ...); no commentary asked.

### 6.5 What I built: `research/cognates.jsonl` and how it was verified
- 309 ancient lemma/root entries (the 19 reference passages of the 2027 syllabus: Φάκελος units 1-18 and 21), 1,853 distinct (lemma, Modern Greek word) pairs accepted, 3 left unchecked. Each line has `lemma`, `forms` (attested in the 2027 reference texts), `units` (Φάκελος numbering), `modern_el`, `modern_el_unchecked`, `verification` (per word), `note` (semantic traps, ΤΘΔΔ item numbers, exam years), `source`.
- Verification (script `research/tools/cognates/verify.py`; build with `build.py`; run 2026-11-02): each modern word's el.wiktionary page was fetched through the MediaWiki API, its etymology section was searched (up to two hops through linked words) for the root stem(s) of the ancient lemma. Result: **1,729** pairs confirmed by el.wiktionary etymology text, **87** accepted as "transparent shared stem" (the modern word visibly contains the stem, e.g. ανυπόδητος from ὑποδέω), **21** taken from the committee's own items (exam papers / ΤΘΔΔ question-bank pairings) and **16** added by hand in `manual_ok.txt` with a reason. Words the automatic check could not support were removed or left out (for example στρατηγός under ἄγω, ίστωρ, ηλιοτρόπιο).
- Coverage: about 73% of the 1,245 distinct word forms of 4+ letters in the 19 passages (particles and articles excluded) match an entry by a loose stem test. The rest are mostly inflected forms of covered lemmas, pronouns, proper names and uncommon words. **It is not a complete lexicon of the texts.**
- Limits: Wiktionary etymology is a proxy for the school tradition (Triantafyllides, Babiniotis); the committee uses remote relatives (τέχνη~τόκος, ποὺς~χταπόδι, προϊόντες~ισθμός) that are marked as exam-attested, and these are not all in Wiktionary. Entries are for *recognition* tasks; when generating "find the cognate in the text" items in the app, the answer must be a word that **actually occurs in the passage of that question**, so use `forms` + `units` to restrict.

## 7. Knowledge cards for the 19 reference passages (own wording; Greek phrases from the passages)

Format: **Idea** (what the passage claims, in the order of the argument) · **Terms** (the book's gloss) · **Asked / likely Β1** · **Devices to cite** · **Parallels and traps**.

**U1 Aristotle, Metaphysics A 2** (used Sept 2022)
- Idea: humans began to philosophise from wonder (θαυμάζειν): first at the odd things at hand (τὰ πρόχειρα τῶν ἀτόπων), then step by step at greater matters (moon, sun, stars, the origin of the universe). Whoever is puzzled (ἀπορῶν) and wonders realises he is ignorant; (so the lover of myths is somehow a philosopher: myths are made of wonders). They philosophised to escape ignorance, so they pursued knowing for its own sake and not for use (οὐ χρήσεώς τινος ἕνεκεν); proof from history: only when necessities and ease/leisure (ῥᾳστώνη, διαγωγή) were available did such reflection begin. Therefore it is the only "free" science: like a free man who exists for himself.
- Terms: θαυμάζω (wonder + curiosity; Plato, Theaetetus 155d same origin), φιλοσοφῶ (love of knowledge; "science of truth"), ἀπορῶ (from ἀ- + πόρος: no way out; aporia = start of inquiry), φρόνησις here = wisdom/knowledge (not the practical virtue of EN), ἐπιστήμη (exact, reasoned knowledge).
- Asked: Sept 2022 Β1 "how Aristotle links θαυμάζειν and φιλοσοφεῖν" (6) + "two linguistic choices showing the historical progression" (4): ἐξ ἀρχῆς μὲν ... εἶτα κατὰ μικρὸν οὕτω προϊόντες (temporal sequence and gradation), participles θαυμάσαντες/διαπορήσαντες, small-to-large objects, articular infinitives (τὸ φεύγειν τὴν ἄγνοιαν, τὸ εἰδέναι τὸ ἐπίστασθαι: abstract nominal style), analogy of the free man.
- Parallels/traps: Kostaras (philosophy = freedom) was the Sept 2022 parallel; "free" here = existing for its own sake (autotelic), not political freedom.

**U2 Aristotle, Protrepticus 8-9** (Sept 2024)
- Idea: bodily and external goods are tools (ὄργανα) whose use is dangerous without the knowledge of right use; so we must desire knowledge, acquire it and use it fittingly. There are sciences that produce, sciences that use, and ruling ones; the supreme one has the right judgment (ὀρθότης τοῦ κρίνειν), uses reason (λόγος), contemplates the whole good (τὸ ὅλον ἀγαθόν): this is philosophy; only it can use all else according to nature (κατὰ φύσιν) and give orders; therefore we must philosophise (φιλοσοφητέον) in order to live as citizens correctly and to spend our life usefully. Addressed to Themison, king of Cyprus.
- Terms: βίος (life as chosen way of living), πολιτεύομαι (live as a citizen), κυρίως ἀγαθόν (the end that is not a means), ἡγεμονικωτέραις (more leading).
- Asked: Sept 2024 Β1: characteristics that make philosophy superior in the second paragraph (ὀρθὴ κρίσις, λόγος, θεωρία τοῦ ὅλου ἀγαθοῦ, ἀναμάρτητος ἐπιτακτικὴ φρόνησις, μόνη ... χρῆσθαι πᾶσι καὶ ἐπιτάττειν).
- Devices: syllogism Εἰ τοίνυν ... φιλοσοφητέον, verbal adjective -τέον (obligation), adverbs (δεόντως, προσηκόντως, εὖ, ὀρθῶς, ὠφελίμως), adjectival participles defining philosophy, hierarchy vocabulary (ὑπηρετοῦσαι/ἐπιτάττουσαι), emphasis (μόνη, ἐκ παντὸς τρόπου).

**U3 Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus 122** (never a reference text in 2021-2026)
- Idea: let neither the young delay nor the old tire of philosophising: no one is too early (ἄωρος) or too late (πάρωρος) for the health of the soul; to say the hour for philosophy has not come/has gone is like saying the hour for eudaimonia has not come/gone; so both: the old to stay young in goods through gratitude for the past, the young to be old through fearlessness of the future; practise what produces eudaimonia (present: we have everything; absent: we do everything to have it).
- Terms: εὐδαιμονία (for Epicurus: pleasure as beginning and end, aponia + ataraxia, reachable by everyone), φιλοσοφεῖν (study of nature + community of friends; aim: therapeutic and instructive), μελετῶ (care, practise), ὥρα (right time).
- Devices: antithetic pairs (νέος/γέρων, ἄωρος/πάρωρος, μήπω/μηκέτι, παρούσης/ἀπούσης, γεγονότα/μέλλοντα), plain vocabulary (no technical terms: practical orientation), imperatives.

**U4 Plato, Protagoras 320c-321b** (Sept 2025 hom.)
- Idea: in mythic time gods existed but not mortal races; when their fated time came the gods shaped them inside the earth from earth and fire; Prometheus and Epimetheus were told to adorn them and distribute capacities; Epimetheus asked to distribute (Prometheus to inspect), gave strength without speed to some, speed to the weak, weapons or another means of safety to others; small ones got flight or underground homes, big ones were protected by size (equalising everything: ἐπανισῶν); his fear: that no species vanish (εὐλάβεια); then protection against mutual destruction and against the seasons (hair, hides), bedding, hooves, differing foods; predators few offspring, prey many (σωτηρία τῷ γένει).
- Terms: εἱμαρμένος χρόνος (fated), γένεσις (divine creation; cf. Timaeus), ὅσα πυρὶ καὶ γῇ κεράννυται (four elements), σῴζω/σωτηρία (survival of kinds: ecological echoes), ἀκόσμητος.
- Asked: Sept 2025 hom. Β1: how Epimetheus distributes to keep balance ("law of compensation").
- Devices: myth opening Ἦν γάρ ποτε χρόνος, repetition of νέμω, balanced antitheses (ἰσχὺν ἄνευ τάχους / ἀσθενεστέρους τάχει), concrete detail lists, vocabulary of care.
- Parallel: Lucretius (Sept 2025). Hesiod/Aeschylus extracts excluded.

**U5 Plato, Protagoras 321b-322a** (Sept 2025 rep.)
- Idea: Epimetheus, not very wise, used up the capacities on the irrational animals; mankind was left unadorned (ἀκόσμητον). Prometheus finds man naked, unshod, unbedded, unarmed on the destined day, in aporia he steals Hephaestus' and Athena's ἔντεχνος σοφία with fire (without fire it could not be acquired or useful) and gives it to man, so man gets the wisdom for life (περὶ τὸν βίον σοφία), but not political art, which was with Zeus (guards too fearsome), so Prometheus slips into the shared workshop of Athena and Hephaestus. Later he was prosecuted for theft through Epimetheus' fault.
- Terms: ἔντεχνος σοφία σὺν πυρί = ἔμπυρος τέχνη (technical knowledge needing fire; homo faber), περὶ τὸν βίον σοφία (practical know-how to survive, not scientific wisdom), πολιτικὴ (τέχνη) (knowledge that makes life in society possible), ἀπορία/εὐπορία. The book quotes Protagoras 318e-319a (his teaching: εὐβουλία περὶ τῶν οἰκείων and περὶ τῶν τῆς πόλεως) to separate household-level and polis-level knowledge.
- Asked: Sept 2025 Β1: how ἔντεχνος σοφία/ἔμπυρος τέχνη relate to περὶ τὸν βίον σοφία and to political virtue (solves survival, cannot found cities).
- Devices: asyndeton of privatives (γυμνόν, ἀνυπόδητον, ἄστρωτον, ἄοπλον: man's lack), parenthetical explanation, narrative tension (ἀπορία), irony toward Epimetheus.

**U6 Plato, Protagoras 322a-323a** (June 2021)
- Idea: man shared in a divine portion → recognises gods, builds altars; he articulates speech and names, invents houses, clothes, food (δημιουργικὴ τέχνη); but scattered, men were killed by beasts (political art incl. war missing); they gathered into cities but wronged one another and scattered again; Zeus, afraid the race would perish, sends Hermes with αἰδώς and δίκη "to be the adornments of cities and bonds of friendship"; Hermes asks whether to distribute like the arts (one doctor for many); Zeus: to all, and a law: kill anyone who cannot share αἰδώς and δίκη as a disease of the city; that is why the Athenians accept anyone's advice on political matters but only experts' on technical ones (political virtue must come through justice and temperance).
- Terms: θεία μοῖρα (divine share; rationality traced to the divine), φωνὴ καὶ ὀνόματα (articulate language), δημιουργικὴ τέχνη (creative craft), πολιτικὴ τέχνη (political art), αἰδώς (respect/shame: inner brake on antisocial behaviour) + δίκη (innate sense of right; not the court case) = σωφροσύνη + δικαιοσύνη, νόσος πόλεως.
- Asked: June 2021: parts of political virtue and their function (Β1); 4 linguistic choices in Zeus' command (νόμον γε θὲς; κτείνειν; ὡς νόσον πόλεως; ἐπὶ πάντας καὶ πάντες μετεχόντων: emphatic repetition) and why the penalty is severe (the incapable endangers the city like a disease) (Β2); parallel Callicles (nature vs convention).
- Link for Β1: contrast technical vs political competence; why aidos+dike are given to all.

**U7 Aristotle, Politics A 1.12** (given in translation; never asked as reference text)
- Idea: natural impulse towards community; the first founder is the cause of the greatest goods; perfected man is the best of animals, separated from law and justice the worst; injustice is most dangerous when armed; man is born with the arms of φρόνησις and virtue, which can serve opposite ends; without virtue most unholy and savage, worst in sex and food; δίκη = order of the political community, δικαιοσύνη = judgement of what is just.
- Terms: φύσει, τελειοῦμαι (τέλος = fulfilment, so only the adult can have full happiness), φρόνησις (here = intellectual ability as a whole), φύεται ἀρετῇ (has the potential to acquire, not innate virtue), δίκη vs δικαιοσύνη.
- Use: compare with U6 (law/justice make humans viable).

**U8 Plato, Republic 514a-515c** (June 2025)
- Idea: allegory of education and its lack: men in an underground cave, long entrance open to light, bound from childhood by legs and necks, looking only ahead; fire behind and above; a road and a low wall like the screens of puppeteers; men carry objects and statues above the wall; prisoners see only the shadows on the wall; if they could talk they would take the shadows for the real things. Glaucon: strange image, strange prisoners; Socrates: they are like us (ὁμοίους ἡμῖν).
- Terms: ἀπεικάζω (marks an allegory: a continued metaphor with another level of meaning), παιδεία/ἀπαιδευσία (thematic; moral formation + knowledge), ἄτοπος (strange; sets up the surprise), τὸ ἀληθές (the truly existing, an ontological sense), πάθος (condition), symbolism: cave = sensible world, prisoners = ordinary people confined to senses/opinion, shackles/immobility = intellectual inertia, shadows = appearances; link with the theory of Ideas.
- Asked: June 2025 Β1: the basic mode of expression (allegory) and its link to lack of education and the prisoners' condition, with quotations (ἀπείκασον, Ἰδὲ, οἷον ἐν καταγείῳ οἰκήσει σπηλαιώδει, ὥσπερ τοῖς θαυματοποιοῖς); Β2: Orwell, one similarity (people accept a fabricated reality imposed by those in power), one difference (Orwell: the Party denies the external world and the senses; Plato: the sensible world is a copy of the real world of Ideas, and the soul must ascend).
- Devices: chain of relative clauses (παρ' ἣν, ὑπὲρ ὧν) building the scene, imperatives ἰδέ/ὅρα engaging the listener, inclusive first plural (ὁμοίους ἡμῖν), light/dark imagery, rhetorical questions of the interlocutor.

**U9 Plato, Republic 518b-519a** (never used)
- Idea: paideia is not what some promise (putting knowledge into a soul that lacks it, like sight into blind eyes); each soul has the power and the organ for learning; like an eye that can turn from dark to light only with the whole body, the whole soul must be turned from becoming toward being and the brightest part of it, the Good; the art of turning (περιαγωγή) does not implant sight but redirects it; the other "virtues" resemble bodily ones (acquired by habit and practice), but phronesis belongs to something more divine: it never loses its power, but becomes useful or harmful depending on its direction.
- Terms: ἀνάμνησις (knowledge is within), τὸ ὄν / τὸ ἀγαθόν (Idea of the Good), περιαγωγή/μεταστροφή (a conversion of the whole being), ἔθος καὶ ἄσκησις, φρόνησις (ambivalent capacity).

**U10 Plato, Republic 519b-520a** (Sept 2026; bank item 28868/28869)
- Idea: neither the uneducated (no single life-goal) nor those left in education who refuse to act (believing they already live on the Isles of the Blessed) can govern; the founders must compel the best natures to the greatest study, see the Good, make the ascent, and then not allow them to stay but make them descend to the prisoners and share toils and honours; Glaucon: injustice? Socrates: you forgot the law's concern: not one class's welfare but the whole city's; it binds citizens by persuasion and compulsion, makes them share benefits each can give, and produces such men to use them for the city's bond, not to let them go their own way.
- Terms: ἀνάβασις/κατάβασις (symbols for the path to knowledge and the return to practical politics), οἰκισταί, νόμος as educator and unifier (συναρμόττων, σύνδεσμος τῆς πόλεως; cf. aidos and dike as "bonds of friendship" in Protagoras), πειθὼ καὶ ἀνάγκη.
- Asked/likely: purpose of the law and the functions it contrives (Sept 2026); who are "οἱ ἐν παιδείᾳ ἐώμενοι" and why Plato criticises them (bank 28869); the parallel was Thucydides' Funeral Oration (bank: differences between Pericles' democracy and Plato's elite) and Plorites (Sept 2026: responsibility of intellectuals).
- Devices: compulsion vocabulary (ἀναγκάσαι, ἀνάγκῃ), participial chain συναρμόττων ... ποιῶν ... ἐμποιῶν (the law as active agent), antithesis οὐχ ἵνα ... ἀλλ' ἵνα, the Isles-of-the-Blessed image.

**U11 Aristotle, Politics Θ 1.3-2.1** (Sept 2022)
- Idea: education must be legislated and made common (κοινή); but what and how is disputed: people disagree on what youths should learn (for virtue or the best life) and whether for intellect or character; the current practice confuses the inquiry; it is unclear whether to train the useful, the virtue-directed, or the "extra" (περιττά); not even virtue is valued the same; the necessary useful things must be taught but not all useful things: distinguish liberal and illiberal works; share in useful things that do not make the learner banausic; banausic = any work, art or study that makes the body or the mind of free men useless for the uses and acts of virtue.
- Terms: παιδεία κοινή (political equality and unity), τρία: διδακτικοί σκοποί / εκπαιδευτική πρακτική / γνωστικό αντικείμενο, περιττά (knowledge without immediate use: cf. Thales, Anaxagoras in EN 1141b), βάναυσον, ἐλευθέριον/ἀνελευθέριον.
- Asked: Sept 2022 Β2: how the aim "education of free citizens" links to the "free science" of Metaphysics A2. The bank (3rd criterion) asks for the three types of education: utilitarian (τὰ χρήσιμα), character-forming (τὰ τείνοντα πρὸς ἀρετήν), knowledge-centred (τὰ περιττά).

**U12 Aristotle, EN Β 1.1-4** (Sept 2023 hom.)
- Idea: two kinds of virtue: intellectual (mostly from teaching; needs experience and time) and moral (from habit: the name ἠθική comes from ἔθος); so no moral virtue arises in us by nature: nothing natural can be habituated otherwise (a stone thrown upward ten thousand times won't rise); virtues arise neither by nor against nature: we are naturally fit to receive them and perfected by habit. With natural capacities we get the capacity first and exercise it later (senses: we saw because we had sight); with virtues we acquire them by acting first (like the arts).
- Terms: φύσει/ἔθει/παρὰ φύσιν, δύναμις-ἐνέργεια (potentiality–actuality; actuality is more important), πρότερον-ὕστερον, τελειοῦμαι.
- Asked: Sept 2023 Β1: content of δύναμις/ἐνέργεια (6) and link with πρότερον/ὕστερον (4). Parallel: Plutarch (Lycurgus' two puppies).

**U13 Aristotle, EN Β 1.5-8** (June 2026, Sept 2023 rep.)
- Idea: lawgivers make citizens good by habituating them; this is every lawgiver's intention, and what distinguishes a good constitution from a bad; virtue and art are produced and destroyed by the same actions: good and bad lyre players from playing; so in builders; if it were otherwise no teacher would be needed; in virtues: acting in dealings with people makes us just or unjust; in dangers, habituating ourselves to fear or confidence makes us brave or cowardly; in desires and anger: temperate and mild vs self-indulgent and irascible; "hexeis" arise from similar activities, so activities must be of a given quality; habituation from youth matters enormously ("everything").
- Terms: νομοθέτης (teacher of citizens), ἔθος, ἕξις, ἐνέργειαι ποιαί, συναλλάγματα (all kinds of transactions: the social base of society), δεινά (feared things), ἐπιθυμία, ὀργή, τέχνη (analogy), ἀνάλογον.
- Asked: June 2026 Β1 (7+3): how arts and virtues arise and perish; the teacher's contribution; Β2 parallel Stathopoulos on justice as «αρετή προς έτερον» vs «Οὕτω δὴ ... ἄδικοι»; Sept 2023 Β1: fields of human action + 2 devices.
- Devices: contrary-to-fact condition (Εἰ γὰρ μὴ οὕτως εἶχεν, οὐδὲν ἂν ἔδει τοῦ διδάξοντος ...) = proof by the opposite; the pair γίνεται–φθείρεται; adverbs εὖ/κακῶς (quality) vs adjectives ἀγαθοί/κακοί (result); polyptoton δίκαια δίκαιοι; paratactic γάρ-chain; list of fields; inductive example.

**U14 Aristotle, EN Β 6.4-8** (Sept 2021)
- Idea: in anything continuous and divisible you can take more, less or equal, in relation to the thing or to us; mean of the thing = equidistant from both extremes, one and the same for all (6 between 10 and 2: arithmetic proportion); mean relative to us = neither too much nor too little, not one, not the same for all; if 10 minas of food are a lot and 2 little, the trainer will not prescribe 6: for Milo it is little, for a beginner much; likewise running and wrestling; so every expert flees excess and deficiency, seeks and chooses the mean, not of the thing but relative to us.
- Terms: συνεχὲς καὶ διαιρετόν (a continuum; here a passion), κατὰ τὸ πρᾶγμα (objective) vs πρὸς ἡμᾶς (subjective: guided by reason and knowledge), ὑπερβολή/ἔλλειψις/μέσον, μέτρον ἄριστον and μηδὲν ἄγαν (Aristotle gives these a rational method).
- Asked: Sept 2021 Β1: the two kinds of mean + two expressive means (concrete numerical example; hypothetical; antithesis ἓν καὶ τὸ αὐτὸ πᾶσιν / οὐχ ἕν οὐδὲ ταὐτὸν πᾶσιν; paratactic narration).
- Trap: the mean is not mediocrity, nor relativism.

**U15 Aristotle, EN Β 6.10-13, 16** (Sept 2021)
- Idea: fear, confidence, desire, anger, pity, pleasure and pain can be felt more or less, both badly; feeling them when, at what, towards whom, for what and as one should is the mean and the best = virtue; the same for actions; virtue concerns passions and actions where excess errs and is blamed, deficiency too, and the mean is praised and hits the target; so virtue is a mean aiming at the mean; definition: ἕξις προαιρετική, ἐν μεσότητι οὖσα τῇ πρὸς ἡμᾶς, ὡρισμένῃ λόγῳ καὶ ᾧ ἂν ὁ φρόνιμος ὁρίσειεν; between two vices (one by excess, one by deficiency); virtue finds and chooses the mean.
- Terms: ἕξις (stable disposition = genus), προαιρετική (rational choice between good and bad, free and conscious: a justice-doer is not whoever happens to do a just act), μεσότης πρὸς ἡμᾶς (subjective, defined by reason), φρόνιμος (the practical model who sets the criterion; the book notes the social element of ἔπαινος/ψόγος), the use of infinitives for passions (action and expression matter more than inner feeling).
- Asked: Sept 2021 Β2: the content of «προαιρετική» (6) and «φρόνιμος» (4); the book's activity: name genus and differentia of the definition.

**U16 Aristotle, Politics Α 1.1-8** (June 2023)
- Idea: every polis is a community aiming at some good; the supreme community, containing all the others, aims at the supreme good = the polis (political community); the community of several villages is the complete polis, having reached the limit of self-sufficiency (αὐτάρκεια); it comes to be for living, exists for living well (τοῦ εὖ ζῆν); hence every polis is by nature (φύσει), as are the first communities; for the polis is their end, and nature is end.
- Terms: κοινωνία, κώμη, οἶκος (household: spouses, parents-children, master-slave), αὐτάρκεια (sufficiency of the goods for happiness: location, human resources for defence, good administration + justice), ζῆν/εὖ ζῆν, τέλος/τέλειος (end/perfection: consistent with teleology), πᾶσα πόλις φύσει ἔστιν (against sophists who made institutions only conventions). The book lists conditions of good life (Pol. 1325b): food, craftsmen, arms, wealth, religion, courts (justice the most important).
- Asked: June 2023 Β1: define the polis and its purpose; genus κοινωνία + differentia (supreme good); stages οἶκος → κώμη → πόλις.
- Trap: the polis ≠ our "city" (it is a state).

**U17 Aristotle, Politics Α 1.10-11** (June 2022)
- Idea: man is a political animal more than any bee or herd animal because nature makes nothing in vain: man alone has logos; voice (φωνή) signals pain/pleasure and animals have it; logos shows the beneficial and the harmful, hence the just and unjust; man alone perceives good/bad/just/unjust; sharing these makes household and polis.
- Terms: τελολογία (οὐθὲν μάτην ἡ φύσις ποιεῖ), λόγος vs φωνή (articulated vs inarticulate; logos = reason + language), ἴδιον (Aristotelian definition: genus ζῷον + differentia λόγον ἔχον), κοινωνία (here: partnership of shared values).
- Asked: June 2022 Β1: the role of nature and logos in characterising man as political; Epictetus (parallel/second text) was Β2.

**U18 Aristotle, Politics Γ 6.3-4** (June 2023)
- Idea: the many may be better judges than the best few, not individually but together (like potluck dinners beating one man's dinner): each has a part of virtue and prudence, and when gathered the multitude is like one man with many feet, hands and senses, in character and mind; so they judge music and poetry better: each a part, all the whole.
- Terms: πλῆθος (the demos, majority), ἄριστοι, κύριον (sovereign), συμφορητὰ δεῖπνα (Aristotle's coinage), "additive" (summative) theory = the root of majority rule.
- Asked: June 2023 Β2: the "superman" (invulnerable, strong, etc.) in the text of Anonymus Iamblichus vs Aristotle's collective "many-footed" man.
- Devices: analogy + example (οἷον ... ὥσπερ ...), cautious optative δόξειεν ἂν, comparative forms (βελτίους, ἄμεινον), conclusion with διό.

**U21 Plutarch, De Alexandri fortuna aut virtute 6, 329A-D** (June 2024)
- Idea: Zeno's much-admired polity aims at one point: not to live in separate cities and demes each with its own laws, but to regard all humans as fellow citizens, one life and one order like a herd feeding under a common law; Zeno wrote it as a dream/image of a philosophic good order, Alexander made it a deed: not following Aristotle's advice (Greeks as leader, barbarians as despot; friends vs plants/animals) that would fill his rule with wars and secret revolts, but believing he was sent by god as common reconciler (ἁρμοστὴς καὶ διαλλακτής); whom he could not unite by word he forced by arms; mixed lives, customs, marriages in a loving cup; ordered all to regard the world as fatherland, his army camp as acropolis, the good as kin and the bad as foreign; Greek and barbarian are distinguished by virtue and vice.
- Terms: εὐνομία (permanent ideal; Stoics: law of nature), πατρίς (redefined by Cynics → cosmopolitanism), οἰκουμένη, κρατὴρ φιλοτήσιος, ἀγέλη συννόμου, αἵρεσις (school of thought, not "heresy").
- Asked: June 2024 Α1 (Σ/Λ with quotations), Β1: two traits Plutarch gives Alexander (divine mission as harmoniser; mixing of peoples by word and arms) and the link to the new idea of fatherland/oikoumene (8 marks); Β2: two similarities between Zeno's polis and Marcus Aurelius (parts of one whole; kinship; common law/reason; social good).
- Traps: anachronism (Zeno acted c. 300 BC, 23 years after Alexander died) and Plutarch's idealisation; Aristotelian ethics fits the polis scale, not the oikoumene.

## 9. What this means for the app (proposals; for the design agent)

1. **Interpretation lessons should train the skill, not retell passages**: each unit gets a "claim chain" (3-5 claims in order, each with a Greek quote). The learner assembles an answer from tiles: thesis → quote → gloss → link. A checker verifies that every claim tile is supported by a quote tile and that the "term" tile matches the book gloss. This matches the official criteria (completeness, clarity, coherence, documentation).
2. **Closed-question trainers**: (a) Σωστό/Λάθος on the introductions with a "flip pair" for each fact (true version / one-word-changed false version, see §3); (b) the Σ/Λ comprehension with a mandatory quotation (3+3 scoring from the bank); (c) the "pronoun reference" mini-game.
3. **Parallel-text drill**: unseen paired passages with the exam's "similarity in para 1, difference in para 2" frame, plus a self-check against the guideline ("did you repeat Β1? did you quote both texts?").
4. **Etymology module** driven by `cognates.jsonl`: the five shapes of §6.2 as question builders (match, find-in-text, fill-in, new-sense, compound); restrict "find in text" answers to forms actually occurring in the passage (`forms` + `units`); show root families (φέρω, ἔχω, λαμβάνω, αἱρέω, βάλλω, λανθάνω, ποιέω, τίθημι, χράομαι, ἄγω, ἵστημι, λείπω, πράττω) as a "root of the day" with 6-8 modern words; flag exam-attested remote relatives (τέχνη~τόκος...) as "level 2".
